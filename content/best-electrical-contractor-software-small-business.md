Detailed notes and how each product fits small electrical contractors

1) Jobber — best low‑cost entry and simple growth path
- What the vendor says: Jobber offers a Core plan intended for solopreneurs (one user) and describes the Core workflow for requests → quotes → jobs → invoices. Jobber also documents Connect, Grow, and Plus tiers that support larger user counts and add features like crew scheduling, job costing, and custom reporting [Jobber Core plan doc](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/) and [Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) (prices retrieved 2026-10-15).
- Why an electrical contractor might choose Jobber: Jobber’s Core plan is explicitly positioned for “solopreneurs with a growing amount of work” and includes online booking, quoting, invoicing, and Jobber Payments; Connect/Grow add automation, time tracking, and job costing as you scale [Jobber Core plan doc](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/). The pricing page lists per‑user add‑ons and multiple plan tiers for incremental upgrades [Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/).
- Notable published fees and payments details: Jobber lists card and ACH processing rates and options on its pricing page and help center — for example, card processing 2.9% + 30¢ (page lists processing rates and instant payout fee options) and ACH at 1%, plus an optional instant payout fee of 1% [Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) (rates retrieved 2026-10-15).
- Drawbacks / when not to pick Jobber: If you need an out‑of‑the‑box price book with benchmarked market pricing, or native advanced AI call intake and multiple AI modules included, Jobber’s published materials indicate those are add‑ons or higher tiers (Receptionist, Marketing Suite) and may increase total cost; check exact add‑on fees. Jobber’s Core plan is limited to one user, so small teams will need Connect or higher and should verify seat costs [Jobber Core plan doc](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/) and [Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/).

2) Housecall Pro — best trade-focused package for small teams who want price books, routing, and built-in AI
- What the vendor says: Housecall Pro publishes Basic, Essentials, and MAX plans with clear price points and user counts (Basic: 1 user at $59/mo billed annually; Essentials: up to 5 users; MAX: up to 8 users at published rates) and a 14‑day free trial [Housecall Pro pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) (prices retrieved 2026-10-15). Housecall Pro markets built-in AI tools (CSR AI, Analyst AI, Marketing AI, Coach AI, Help AI) and trade‑specific Default Price Books (including a deeper set for electrical) [Housecall Pro FSM overview](https://www.housecallpro.com/field-service-management-software/) and [Price Book doc](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/9778163-how-to-use-price-book-by-housecall-pro).
- Why an electrical contractor might choose Housecall Pro: The vendor provides Default Price Books tailored to electrical work (pre‑loaded services and homeowner‑friendly descriptions) and a Price Book that can be customized — helpful if you want quick flat‑rate or service templates to estimate jobs faster [Housecall Pro price book doc](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/9778163-how-to-use-price-book-by-housecall-pro). Housecall Pro also highlights route optimization, GPS tracking, and an AI call/chat intake (CSR AI) that can reduce missed leads [Housecall Pro FSM overview](https://www.housecallpro.com/field-service-management-software/).
- Drawbacks / when not to pick Housecall Pro: Housecall Pro’s higher tiers and some advanced functions (e.g., Pricing Insights) are gated to the MAX plan or mechanical package per the vendor docs. If you need a very low monthly outlay for a one‑person business, Jobber’s Core plan lists a lower starting published price than Housecall Pro’s Basic annual price; check annual vs. monthly billing and trial options before deciding [Housecall Pro pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) and [Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/) (prices retrieved 2026-10-15).
- Vendor marketing claims to note: Housecall Pro presents metrics such as “Trusted by 200,000+ Pros” and vendor‑reported averages (e.g., “35% avg. revenue growth after the first year” and “8+ avg. hours saved per week”) — these are vendor claims and should be verified for your use case [Housecall Pro pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) and [Housecall Pro FSM overview](https://www.housecallpro.com/field-service-management-software/).

3) ServiceTitan — best when you plan to scale into a larger, process-driven business
- What the vendor says: ServiceTitan publishes product packages and positions itself for commercial and residential contractors, but its public page asks prospects to request pricing or a demo; ServiceTitan uses a per‑technician pricing model and emphasizes advanced features such as Atlas, CRM, project management, pricebook, reporting, and accounting modules [ServiceTitan pricing](https://www.servicetitan.com/pricing) (contact sales).
- Why an electrical contractor might choose ServiceTitan: Vendor materials emphasize enterprise features (in‑depth reporting, payroll, configurable memberships, and a large feature set) and position ServiceTitan for businesses that want a full operational platform that grows with team size. The vendor claims a broad contractor customer base and positions the product for growth [ServiceTitan pricing](https://www.servicetitan.com/pricing).
- Drawbacks / when not to pick ServiceTitan: Public pricing is not published in the packet — ServiceTitan requires a demo/request‑a‑quote process; smaller shops that want predictable self‑service pricing and a short free trial may prefer vendors with published plans (see Housecall Pro and Jobber). If you are a solopreneur or a 1–3 person shop, validate total per‑tech licensing + onboarding costs before budgeting [ServiceTitan pricing](https://www.servicetitan.com/pricing).

Short decision framework (practical steps)
1. Map your must‑have features: number of users, price book/templates, payment processing, QuickBooks sync, routing/GPS, and AI call intake.
2. Set a 12‑month budget that includes per‑user fees, add‑ons (receptionist/AI, marketing, price book premiums), onboarding, and processing fees.
3. Trial the vendors: Housecall Pro and Jobber both offer trials per their pages (Housecall Pro 14‑day; Jobber’s Core trial note is documented) — load a handful of real jobs and run through quoting → scheduling → invoicing with your typical workflows [Housecall Pro pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) and [Jobber Core plan doc](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/).
4. Verify two integrations you can’t live without (QuickBooks, pricebook or supplier integration) during the trial to see how data flows.
5. Ask sales for a written total cost (software + required add‑ons + estimated processing fees) and a demo of reporting for your KPIs.
