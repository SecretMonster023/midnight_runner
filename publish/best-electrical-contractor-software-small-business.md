## What each vendor offers and when to pick it

### Jobber — entry-level, trade-friendly growth path
- What the vendor says: Jobber positions its Core plan for solopreneurs (one user) and documents higher tiers (Connect, Grow, Plus) that add scheduling, job costing and more; see the [Jobber Core plan doc](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/) and [Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/).
- Why electricians choose it: Core supports the basic quote → schedule → invoice workflow and integrates Jobber Payments and QuickBooks sync per vendor docs, with documented paths to add users/features as you grow.
- Drawbacks / when not to pick Jobber: Core is limited to one user; features like Receptionist or Marketing Suite are add‑ons or higher tiers. If you need an out‑of‑the‑box electrical price book or built‑in AI intake at base price, those capabilities may require upgrades or extras — verify before buying.

### Housecall Pro — trade‑focused price books, routing, and vendor AI
- What the vendor says: Housecall Pro publishes Basic, Essentials, and MAX plans with user counts and a 14‑day trial; it also provides Default Price Books for trades including electrical and markets built‑in AI tools and routing features — see [Housecall Pro pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) and the [Price Book doc](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/9778163-how-to-use-price-book-by-housecall-pro).
- Why electricians choose it: Vendor‑provided Default Price Books (editable) speed quoting for common electrical services; routing/GPS and CSR AI support intake and dispatch workflows.
- Drawbacks / when not to pick Housecall Pro: Some advanced capabilities (e.g., Pricing Insights and other higher‑tier features) are gated to top plans. Also check annual vs. monthly billing math if you need the absolute lowest monthly outlay.
- Vendor marketing claims to note: Housecall Pro makes vendor‑reported claims such as “Trusted by 200,000+ Pros” and customer outcome averages (e.g., revenue/efficiency improvements); those are vendor claims and should be validated for your business on the linked pages ([Housecall Pro pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) / [Housecall Pro FSM overview](https://www.housecallpro.com/field-service-management-software/)).

### ServiceTitan — enterprise features for scaling businesses
- What the vendor says: ServiceTitan positions itself for larger residential/commercial contractors and uses a per‑technician pricing model; public materials ask prospects to request pricing/demo ([ServiceTitan pricing](https://www.servicetitan.com/pricing)).
- Why electricians choose it: Vendor materials emphasize deep reporting, advanced pricebook and project features, payroll/accounting integrations and broad configurability suited to process‑driven expansion.
- Drawbacks / when not to pick ServiceTitan: No list pricing on the public page in the packet — you must request a demo/quote. Smaller shops wanting predictable, self‑serve pricing and a short free trial may prefer vendors with published plans.

## What to verify before you buy
- Exact monthly vs. annual billing math and the total published price for the number of users you need; confirm per‑user add‑on costs where applicable ([Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/), [Housecall Pro pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/)).
- Which features are included vs. add‑ons (e.g., Jobber Receptionist/Marketing Suite; Housecall Pro Pricing Insights or mechanical packages) — verify feature availability on your intended plan ([Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/), [Housecall Pro Price Book doc](https://help.housecallpro.com/en/articles/9778163-how-to-use-price-book-by-housecall-pro)).
- Payment processing rates, ACH options and instant payout fees (vendors publish example rates and options; confirm current merchant underwriting and regional variations) — check processor details on each pricing page ([Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/), [Housecall Pro pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/)).
- QuickBooks and accounting sync behavior (one‑way vs. two‑way, mapping of payments and fees) and whether any integration fees apply ([Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/), [Housecall Pro FSM overview](https://www.housecallpro.com/field-service-management-software/)).
- Onboarding and support levels (white‑glove or dedicated onboarding may be limited to higher tiers); contract terms and cancellation policies — confirm current terms on the vendor pages.
- Any vendor marketing claims you plan to rely on (customer counts, average outcomes): treat these as vendor‑reported and validate via trial or references ([Housecall Pro pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/)).

## Short decision framework (practical steps)
1. List must‑have features: number of users, price book/templates, payment options, QuickBooks sync behavior, routing/GPS, and intake (AI or CSR).  
2. Build a 12‑month budget: include base subscription, per‑user fees, required add‑ons, onboarding, and processing fees.  
3. Trial the workflows: load a few real jobs and run quoting → scheduling → dispatch → invoicing. Housecall Pro lists a 14‑day trial on its pricing page; Jobber documents its Core workflow in the help center ([Housecall Pro pricing](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/) and [Jobber Core plan doc](https://help.getjobber.com/en/articles/the-core-plan/)).  
4. Verify integrations you can’t live without (QuickBooks and your price book or supplier data) during the trial.  
5. Ask sales for a written total‑cost quote (software + required add‑ons + estimated processing fees) and a sample of the reports you’ll need.
