## Verdict

For solo HVAC owners who want a simple, low-cost path from quotes to payments, Jobber’s Core or Connect plans are the easiest fit. For 1–10 tech crews that want the most trade-tailored, ready-made HVAC price book and AI helpers, Housecall Pro is the strongest out-of-the-box option. If you need enterprise modules, deep reporting, and per-technician licensing as you scale, get a demo and a written quote from ServiceTitan.

### Quick orientation
- Jobber: simple, low-friction setup for solo and small crews; clear plan pages and add-user options. See Jobber pricing and Core/Connect docs.
- Housecall Pro: trade-focused defaults (HVAC price book), route optimization, and vendor-published AI tools; plan user counts shown on the vendor page.
- ServiceTitan: modular, per-technician licensing and heavy customization; pricing is quote-driven — contact Sales for exact per-tech costs.

Who each product fits — size, trade, and buying priorities

### Jobber — best when you want simple, affordable core workflows
Use Jobber if you run a one-person business or a very small crew and your primary needs are online booking, quoting, invoicing, and integrated payments. Jobber’s Core plan is designed for solopreneurs and the Connect plan supports small teams; Jobber shows add-user pricing on its pricing page. See Jobber pricing and the Core and Connect plan pages for details.

Meaningful drawbacks
- Several capabilities are add-ons (Receptionist, Marketing Suite, Pipeline). Confirm total add-on costs for the features you need. See Jobber pricing for the list of add-ons.
- If you require enterprise-grade, highly customized workflows or multi-location management, you may outgrow Jobber; confirm custom/franchise options with Sales.

### Housecall Pro — best when you want HVAC-ready price books and AI helpers
Use Housecall Pro if your 1–10 tech crew wants a ready-made HVAC price book, route optimization, vehicle GPS, and multiple AI features that the vendor highlights. Housecall Pro lists plan tiers with included user counts (Basic, Essentials, MAX) and documents its Price Book tools. See Housecall Pro pricing and the Price Book documentation.

Meaningful drawbacks
- The vendor markets several AI helpers and outcome stats; treat those as vendor claims and confirm which AI tools are included on your chosen plan. See Housecall Pro’s field service overview and pricing for plan-specific details.
- API access and some advanced features are tied to higher-tier plans; verify whether MAX or a custom plan is required for the API and Pricing Insights.

### ServiceTitan — best when you need enterprise modules and per-tech pricing
Use ServiceTitan if your priorities include advanced dispatching, accounting, payroll, campaign attribution, and highly configurable reporting. ServiceTitan groups features into packages and uses a per-technician pricing model; the vendor requires a demo to provide a tailored quote. See ServiceTitan pricing for package descriptions and to request a demo.

Meaningful drawbacks
- ServiceTitan does not publish per-technician prices on its public pricing page in the research packet — you must request a proposal to compare total cost for 1–10 techs.
- For sole operators or teams wanting a fixed, self-serve monthly price, ServiceTitan’s demo-and-quote sales model can be slower and more involved.

What to verify before you buy
- ServiceTitan: exact per-technician monthly price, setup/onboarding fees, contract length, and which modules are included vs add-ons. Ask for a written quote.
- User licensing: how the vendor defines a “user” (mobile technician app vs office dispatcher) and whether extra users are per-seat or tier steps.
- Payment processing: card and ACH rates, payout timing, and how processing fees post to your accounting system.
- Accounting sync: confirm how job line items, taxes, fees, and payments map into QuickBooks (or your accounting package) on a test/staging account.
- Trial/demo coverage: check which plan level and modules are enabled during the trial. Ask specifically whether AI tools, API access, and price book items are available in the trial.

Practical decision checklist (use during your trial or demo)
- Must-haves list: write down your non-negotiables (examples: 2-way SMS, route optimization, HVAC price book items, QuickBooks sync).
- Match must-haves to plan tiers: map each must-have to the vendor plan and confirm whether it’s included or an add-on.
- Run a live workflow during the trial: create a quote from a site visit, convert to a scheduled job, capture field photos/notes, invoice, and collect payment.
- Test accounting sync: verify one job’s full accounting flow in a staging QuickBooks file.
- Get onboarding details in writing: hours included, support channels, and any extra fees for setup or data migration.
- Obtain a written total cost: monthly fees, per-user or per-tech charges, payment processing fees, and any onboarding or minimum-contract commitments.

Final, practical recommendations
- Solo/owner-operator: start Jobber Core’s trial to validate quoting, invoicing, and payments. See Jobber Core and Jobber pricing.
- Small crew (2–5 techs) wanting HVAC price books and routing: trial Housecall Pro Essentials and evaluate the Price Book and CSR/AI features. See Housecall Pro pricing and the Price Book doc.
- Planning to scale beyond 8–10 techs or needing enterprise modules: request a ServiceTitan demo and a written per-tech quote to compare total cost and modules. See ServiceTitan pricing.

What to verify right away with vendors
- Confirm exact per-user/per-tech cost and onboarding fees.
- Confirm which AI tools and API access are included on the plan you’ll use.
- Confirm payment-processing rates for your merchant region and how fees post to accounting.
